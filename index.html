    How your results work

    The quiz: two parts

    The quiz has 30 questions in two parts, each reported separately.

    • Part 1, Your natural work style (23 questions). In each workplace scenario you choose the response that is most like you and a different response that is least like you, based on your first instinct. Each scenario has one response for each of the four styles, shown in a shuffled order, and no response is meant to be better than another. All 23 scenarios together produce your Color DNA percentages.
    • Part 2, How you like to connect (7 questions). You choose what you appreciate most from colleagues, such as how you like to receive feedback, instructions and updates. The options describe kinds of communication, not colors. Results are counts, and they are never added to Part 1.

    The scoring (Part 1)

    • Net score. For each style: the number of times you marked it “most” minus the number of times you marked it “least”. Net scores run from −23 to +23 and always add up to 0.
    • Chart percentages. Only for drawing the ring, we add 23 to each net score so nothing is negative, then divide by 92. A perfectly even profile is 25% each and one style can reach at most 50%. Nothing is amplified, boosted, or forced, and rounding keeps the four numbers at exactly 100%.
    • Labels. We compare the two highest net scores. A gap of 5 or more is described as a strongest observed preference, a gap of 1 to 4 as a closely matched pair, a gap of 0 as a tie, and a very flat profile (highest minus lowest of 5 or less) as evenly balanced. A second style is named only when it is at least 5 points above the remaining styles; otherwise we say the other styles are not clearly separated. These cut-offs are provisional product rules for wording, not scientific thresholds. They were set for 23 questions by scaling the earlier 15-question cut-offs with the square root of the length, because random noise grows with the square root of the number of questions.

    Why this format

    An earlier version asked for one answer per question and gave every answer points to two styles, with the second style fixed in advance. Choosing a “most” and a “least” response gives two signals per question, asks you to weigh real trade-offs, and removes the built-in link between neighboring styles. It does not make differences between styles look bigger: a style’s share can only range from 0% to 50%, and we have not shown that it ranks people better than single-choice questions would.

    What it can and cannot tell you

    • It can show which kinds of responses you preferred, relative to your other options, in the 23 Part 1 scenarios, and give you a starting point for a conversation. Part 2 describes what you said you appreciate from colleagues.
    • It cannot show how strong a style is compared with other people, how you behave in practice, or who you are. Because you always rank within the same four styles, a high score in one means a lower score in others. The assessment is a small sample, it cannot reliably separate natural from learned behavior, answers shift with role, mood, and context, and people sometimes choose what sounds best. The assessment has not been validated against external measures.
    • Do not use it for hiring, promotion, or other decisions about people.

    Working with the four colors

    The “Connect with others” tips use a simple four-style framework based on the widely used DISC approach, with a color shorthand. They are habits to try, not rules, and no pairing is better or worse than another.
